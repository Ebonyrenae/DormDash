# DORMDASH 🏃
DORMDASH is an online student marketplace where students can post a service they need done and other students can complete that posted service at a posted or negotiated price.

## Technologies
* TypeScript
* CSS
* HTML
* PHP
* MySQL
* Figma

## Features
What students can do with DORMDASH:
* **Post a service:** A student posts a service by choosing the service type from the available options, stating the location, budget, time and date, and an optional short description.
* **Accept a service:** A student can click any job from the All Jobs page, see the details of the posted job, and then accept it.
* **Negotiate a price:** Once a job has been accepted, the student can negotiate a price through their Your Jobs section by clicking "Negotiate Price" and entering a new price point with an optional message for the job poster. They will then be notified whether the price was accepted or declined.
* **Accept or decline a negotiated price:** The job poster has the choice of accepting or declining a negotiated price sent by the job acceptor.
* **Notifications:** Once a job has been accepted, the notification center sends a notification showing who accepted the job.
* **My Requests:** A page where students can see and access all their Active, In Progress, and Completed jobs.
* **Your Jobs:** A page where students can access their Accepted, In Progress, and Completed jobs. This is also where they can cancel a job, negotiate a price, start a job, and mark a job as completed.
* **Calendar:** A monthly view of all accepted and posted jobs.
* **Dashboard:** Includes the notification center icon, upcoming jobs, recently viewed jobs, calendar icon, and sidebar.

## The Process
This was a class project with a team of three students in a software agile development course. We started by designing the different pages on Figma. In this class everything was done in sprints and features were predetermined by our Project Manager after we listed them through the PM tool for our class.

Every week we would meet twice with the Project Manager, and each day we would be assigned tasks for features with three days to complete them. Our process during those three days began with writing user stories and task cards — for user stories we had to write Acceptance Tests and Alternate Path tests, and for task cards we wrote backend tests using Postman or frontend tests in dev tools.

Once a task had all its tests, the card was moved to the In Progress list once we started coding. Next came testing, where you had to run through all the tests created for that task — all of them had to pass. In our meetings we would demo the feature to our Project Manager, who would then move the card to the Complete list on the Scrumboard, and once confirmed, to Closed.

At the end of every sprint we had to deploy our code to both a production website and a testing website to be judged and tested by other Project Managers and our instructors.

## What I Learned
I learned a lot from this project both as a developer and in working with people.

1. **Backend development:** This was my first project building an actual backend without using a third-party service like Firebase, which I used in my first application, Occupancy. I learned how it all works — using PHP and MySQL, how the frontend communicates with the backend, and how to work with servers and databases.
2. **New languages:** Through this process I learned PHP, MySQL, and TypeScript, which I had no experience with beforehand.
3. **Databases:** I learned not only how to create databases, but how to design them effectively for specific purposes and how to think about relationships between them.
4. **The process:** The agile development side of this project was probably the hardest but most eye-opening part. I learned how much more difficult it is to follow a structured process, but also how beneficial it is — and how straying from it doesn't only affect the product but also the people on your team. There were many times where a mistake, whether mine or someone else's, came back to bite us later.
5. **Collaborating:** A big takeaway was learning how to share ideas, accept input from others, and consider your teammates in every step — whether that's with your time, GitHub commits, or merging branches — because your actions directly affect the team.
6. **Working under pressure:** There were many times where I or the whole team were under a time crunch and had to deliver a lot with very little time. On top of that, our team went through significant challenges — we went from a team of five to three within the first two weeks, and then down to two closer to the second-to-last sprint. It was difficult, but we adjusted and pushed through to get the app to a working state by the end of the semester.

## Overall Growth
